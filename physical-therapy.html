🦵 Physical therapy

Milestones, movement, and confidence on the playground.

0–18 typical age range
45–60 min session length
In-network with 9 plans

Pediatric physical therapy looks like play because that is how children learn movement: by playing hard and often. Underneath the games is a clinical eye watching quality, symmetry, and progression.

Our PT lead is a board-certified pediatric clinical specialist (PCS) with sixteen years across early intervention, NICU follow-up, and school-age orthopedics. The rest of the team carries that standard.

What a plan covers

Inside physical therapy at Tidepool

Baby milestones

Tummy time troubles, rolling, sitting, crawling, and first steps. Early intervention works, and the earlier visit is the easier fix.

Torticollis & plagiocephaly

Head-turn preference and flat spots, treated with positioning, stretching, and home programming that fits into diaper changes, not separate appointments.

Toe-walking & gait

Idiopathic toe-walking, in-toeing, and awkward gait patterns, assessed honestly: some resolve alone, and we will tell you when watching is the right treatment.

Coordination & balance

The child who falls harder and more often than peers, or avoids climbing equipment everyone else uses.

Return to play

Post-injury and post-cast programs for school-age athletes and playground casualties, rebuilt to full confidence rather than bare tolerance.

Hypermobility & posture

Joint hypermobility, low muscle tone, and the posture habits that follow them, with strengthening programs kids actually complete.

Is this the right door?

Who we typically see

  • Your baby is not rolling by six months or sitting by nine
  • A strong head-turn preference or a flattening spot on the skull
  • Toe-walking persisting past age three
  • Your school-age child avoids stairs, bikes, or playground equipment
  • Returning to sport after a sprain or fracture
  • Grandma says 'he'll grow out of it' and your gut disagrees

Not sure your child fits these? The free consult exists exactly for that question. Twenty minutes, honest answer.

Our evidence baseline

  • Early intervention referral pathway, including county Regional Center coordination
  • Standardized milestone measures at every visit, trended across time
  • Home programs embedded in existing routines: bath time, diaper changes, playground trips
  • Honest observation periods where treatment would add nothing but cost

We track each child's progress with the same measures every visit and show you the graph at every plan review. If the line is flat for twelve weeks, we change the plan, not the explanation.

Common questions

Physical therapy FAQ

Do we need a referral? +

No. California gives families direct access to therapy evaluations. If your insurance plan requires a physician referral for reimbursement, we will tell you during the benefits check and help you get one quickly.

How often will we come in? +

Most families start with one 45–60 minute session per week. Kids who need faster progress often do two. We re-check frequency at every twelve-week plan review rather than letting it drift.

What do sessions actually look like? +

Play-based and purposeful. Your child will experience it as games, obstacles, and stories. You will experience it as a clinician who can name exactly which skill they were targeting and how you can practice it at home in five minutes a day.

Can you work with my child's school? +

Yes. With your written OK we share goals and progress notes with teachers, join IEP meetings by request, and coach school staff on the same strategies we use in the clinic so the approach is consistent everywhere.

What if my child refuses to participate? +

That is information, not failure. Early sessions may look like relationship-building: showing your child the room, the toys, and the person. We would rather spend two sessions earning trust than one session forcing compliance.
